## Engine RPM
Engine RPM is how many times the crankshaft turns per minute. At idle a warm petrol engine usually turns slowly and steadily; RPM rises with throttle. An idle that hunts up and down, or that is much higher than usual once the engine is warm, can point to a vacuum leak, a dirty throttle body, a faulty idle control or a sensor fault. Very low RPM together with low oil pressure is a common pattern in readings from engines that need attention, because oil pump output falls with engine speed.

## Lubricant oil pressure
Oil pressure is the pressure the oil pump creates to push oil through the bearings, camshaft and other moving parts. Pressure is lowest at hot idle and rises with RPM. Low oil pressure is one of the most serious warnings an engine gives: it can mean the oil level is low, the oil is too thin or degraded, the oil filter or pickup is blocked, the pump is worn, or the bearings are worn. If the oil pressure warning light comes on while driving, stop the engine as soon as it is safe and check the oil level; driving on with no oil pressure can destroy the engine within minutes. Unusually high oil pressure can come from oil that is too thick, a stuck pressure-relief valve or a blocked passage.

## Fuel pressure
Fuel pressure is the pressure the fuel pump supplies to the injectors. Too little pressure starves the engine: symptoms include hard starting, hesitation under load, misfires and loss of power. Common causes are a weak fuel pump, a clogged fuel filter, a faulty pressure regulator or a leak. Too much pressure makes the mixture rich, which can cause poor fuel economy, black smoke and fouled spark plugs; a stuck regulator or a blocked return line are typical causes. Any smell of fuel or visible fuel leak is a fire risk and needs a mechanic before the vehicle is driven.

## Coolant pressure
The cooling system runs under pressure so the coolant can get hotter than its normal boiling point without boiling. The radiator or expansion-tank cap holds that pressure. Low coolant pressure can mean a leak, a faulty cap or low coolant level; the engine may then overheat. Pressure that rises quickly, or bubbles in the expansion tank, can indicate combustion gas entering the coolant, which is a sign of a failing head gasket. Never open the radiator or expansion-tank cap while the engine is hot: the coolant can spray out and scald.

## Lubricant oil temperature
Oil temperature shows how hot the engine oil is. Oil that runs hotter than usual thins out and protects the engine less, and it ages faster. High oil temperature often follows from cooling problems, heavy load or towing, a low oil level, or an oil cooler that is blocked. Oil that stays cold for a long time, for example on very short trips, does not burn off moisture and fuel, which also degrades it. Changing oil and the oil filter at the intervals in the owner's manual is the main defence.

## Coolant temperature
Coolant temperature is the engine's main health gauge. After warm-up it should stay near the middle of the gauge. A reading that climbs above normal means the engine is overheating; likely causes are low coolant, a leak, a thermostat stuck closed, a failed water pump, a blocked radiator or a cooling fan that does not switch on. If the temperature warning light comes on, stop when safe, switch off the engine and let it cool before checking the coolant level. A reading that stays low can mean a thermostat stuck open, which hurts fuel economy and heater output.

## Oil versus coolant temperature
Oil and coolant temperatures normally rise together and settle close to each other once the engine is warm. A large gap between them, or one rising while the other stays flat, suggests a problem in one circuit only, such as a blocked oil cooler, a sticking thermostat or a faulty sensor.

## Reading an AutoCare risk score
The AutoCare fault-risk score is a statistical estimate from a model trained on snapshot sensor readings. It is not an inspection and it cannot see wear, leaks or noises. Treat a high score as a prompt to check the readings it highlights, starting with oil level and coolant level, and have a qualified mechanic inspect the engine before relying on the vehicle.
